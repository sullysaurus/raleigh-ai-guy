Granola is excellent for meetings. It is less helpful with yogurt.

**Granola is an AI meeting notepad that listens to a live conversation, combines the transcript with anything you type, and turns the result into organized notes.** It runs on your computer or iPhone instead of sending a visible bot into the meeting.

The best part is not automatic transcription. Plenty of tools can produce a wall of words. Granola treats the few rough notes you write as signals about what mattered, then uses the wider conversation to fill in the useful context.

## How Granola works

Connect your calendar, open an upcoming meeting in Granola, and start the note when the meeting begins. For an unscheduled conversation, create a Quick Note.

During the meeting, Granola captures your microphone and system audio. You can leave the page blank, but jotting down a phrase such as “pricing concern,” “launch date,” or “ask Sarah” guides what the enhanced notes emphasize.

When the meeting ends, Granola uses:

- The live transcript
- Your rough notes
- Information from the calendar event

to produce a structured version. Its [AI-enhanced notes documentation](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/ai-enhanced-notes) also lets you inspect where a point came from in the transcript or your notes.

That source trail matters. An AI summary should help you review the meeting, not become an unquestionable record of what happened.

## No meeting bot

Granola’s desktop application captures audio from your microphone and computer, so it works with calls in Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack, and other applications without adding “Granola Notetaker” as another attendee.

According to its [transcription guide](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription), capture starts when you open the relevant meeting note or Quick Note. The desktop or iPhone app is required to capture a meeting; the web interface is for viewing and editing existing notes.

No visible bot makes meetings feel less cluttered. It does **not** remove your obligation to tell people you are transcribing them. More on that shortly.

## Why your rough notes make it better

Most meeting assistants decide what matters after the meeting. Granola lets your attention shape the output while the conversation is happening.

Suppose a customer says:

> The reporting is good, but getting our data into the system before Monday could be difficult.

You type only:

```text
onboarding risk — data before Monday
```

Granola can use the transcript to expand that shorthand into a useful note with the surrounding context. You remain present instead of trying to type every sentence, but you still tell the AI where to focus.

The [note-taking guide](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/taking-notes-in-granola) says headings, bullets, checkboxes, and images can also help shape the enhanced result. You can leave the note blank, but even three human-selected anchors can make the summary more relevant.

## What can you do with the notes?

After a meeting, you can edit the summary, copy it as Markdown, share it with specific people, or send it to supported tools. Granola currently documents integrations including Slack, Notion, HubSpot, Affinity, Attio, and Zapier.

You can also chat with one meeting or a group of meetings. Useful questions include:

- What objections came up across customer calls this month?
- Which commitments did I make in this meeting?
- What decisions changed between the first and final project calls?
- Draft a follow-up email using the agreed next steps.
- Turn these interviews into a list of recurring customer language.

Chat is useful for recall and synthesis. It is not a reason to skip verifying a quote, commitment, price, or deadline against the source.

## Where Granola is most useful

Granola is a strong fit for:

- Customer discovery and sales calls
- Recruiting interviews
- Project planning and status meetings
- One-on-ones
- Partner conversations
- In-person meetings captured through the iPhone app
- Anyone who wants to pay attention without losing the details

It is especially valuable when the meeting creates decisions and follow-up. If nobody will use the notes again, automating them creates a tidy archive rather than a business result.

## How to set it up well

### 1. Connect the right calendar

Use the calendar containing the meetings you want Granola to recognize. Review the requested access and your company’s policy before connecting a work account.

### 2. Configure audio and language

Run a low-stakes test call. Confirm Granola can hear both you and the other side, especially if you switch between laptop speakers, a dock, a headset, and external microphones.

Add company names, product terms, and industry language to the available jargon settings. A transcript that consistently misspells the central product makes every later summary harder to trust.

### 3. Create one useful template

Do not start with ten templates. Build one around a recurring meeting.

For a customer call:

```text
# What they are trying to accomplish
# Current process
# Friction and objections
# Exact customer language
# Decisions
# Next actions and owners
```

### 4. Take anchor notes

Write decisions, surprises, commitments, and moments you want to revisit. Do not recreate the transcript manually.

### 5. End the meeting deliberately

Stop Granola when the conversation ends. Its documentation notes that an active microphone or an over-running meeting can cause sessions to merge if capture is not stopped.

### 6. Review before sharing

Correct names, owners, dates, numbers, and commitments. Remove side conversations or sensitive material that recipients do not need.

## Consent and privacy

Because Granola does not join as a visible bot, other participants may not know it is running.

Tell them.

Granola’s [consent guide](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/getting-consent) recommends asking for consent before transcribing and provides consent-messaging options for some workflows. Consent requirements differ by location and context, so follow the rules that apply to every participant—not simply the meeting host.

Granola says desktop audio is processed for transcription but not retained as an audio recording; iPhone audio may be temporarily cached until transcription completes. The transcript and notes do persist. Its [security and privacy FAQ](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs) explains current storage, sharing, and model-training controls.

Notes are private by default, but a shared link changes who can see them. Review the sharing setting every time, particularly for interviews, personnel conversations, health information, legal topics, or confidential customer work.

## What Granola does not do perfectly

- It can misunderstand names, accents, and specialized language.
- Desktop transcripts may identify speakers broadly as “Me” and “Them” rather than naming every person.
- It is designed for live meetings, not uploading a library of old recordings.
- A clean summary may hide uncertainty or disagreement in the actual conversation.
- Capturing every meeting can create more information than anyone will review.
- It cannot know which commitment is safe or realistic for your business.

The tool improves memory. It does not replace judgment.

## Should you use Granola?

Use Granola if taking notes prevents you from participating fully, if important follow-up regularly disappears, or if patterns across conversations would help you make better decisions.

Skip it when the meeting is too sensitive to transcribe, participants have not consented, or the conversation does not produce information anyone needs later.

The ideal result is not perfect notes from every meeting. It is fewer forgotten commitments, better customer understanding, and more attention available for the person on the other side of the call.

And yes, you can still eat regular granola afterward.
