Claude can feel like six products hiding inside one chat box.

You hear about Projects, instructions, Skills, connectors, Claude Design, and Cowork. A simple question—“How can this help my business?”—quickly turns into a software vocabulary lesson.

You do not need to learn everything at once.

In one focused hour, you can set up Claude around a real business task, give it useful context, create reusable instructions, and decide whether any advanced feature is worth adding. This bootcamp is designed for nontechnical owners who want finished work, not another technology hobby.

## The plain-English map

Here is the entire Claude product in business terms:

| Claude feature | Plain-English job |
|---|---|
| **Chat** | Complete one task or think through one problem |
| **Project** | Keep the background material for one area of the business together |
| **Project instructions** | Tell Claude how to work inside that Project |
| **Claude Design** | Turn an approved brief into static visuals, decks, or prototypes |
| **Skill** | Save a repeatable procedure Claude can use when relevant |
| **Connector** | Give Claude approved access to another service or source |
| **Cowork** | Delegate a larger, multi-step outcome with a clear finish line |

That is the progression: **one task, one area of context, one repeatable process, then careful delegation.**

## Minutes 0–5: choose one real task

Do not begin with “How can I use AI?” Choose work you already need to finish this week.

Good first tasks include:

- Drafting a customer follow-up from your notes
- Turning a meeting transcript into decisions and action items
- Creating a one-page service overview
- Summarizing customer feedback
- Preparing a weekly operating update
- Repurposing one useful idea into email and social content

Avoid high-stakes decisions, sensitive data, irreversible actions, or a vague request to “run my business.” A first task should be easy to recognize when finished and easy for you to check.

## Minutes 5–15: create your first Project

A Claude Project is a workspace for an ongoing area of work. Its knowledge and instructions can be reused across chats inside that Project.

For example, create a Project called **Marketing** and add:

- A plain-English description of the business
- Your audience and service area
- Current offers and pricing you have approved
- Three to five examples of writing that sounds like you
- A brand guide or approved logo files
- Frequently asked questions
- Required disclaimers or review rules

Anthropic’s [Projects guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9519177-how-can-i-create-and-manage-projects) explains how to add project knowledge and instructions. One detail matters: chats inside a Project do not automatically share everything discussed in other chats. Put reusable information in the Project knowledge or instructions.

Do not upload every file you own. Add the smallest useful set of current, approved material. Keep sensitive customer, employee, legal, financial, and licensed information out unless your organization has approved that specific use.

## Minutes 15–25: learn one prompt structure

A good business prompt does not need special syntax. It needs six parts:

1. **Outcome:** What finished result do you need?
2. **Context:** What should Claude know about the situation?
3. **Source:** Which facts or files may it use?
4. **Constraints:** What must it preserve, avoid, or limit?
5. **Format:** What should the finished work look like?
6. **Review:** What must Claude flag for a human?

Compare these two requests:

> Write a follow-up email.

> Draft a follow-up email to a customer who requested an estimate yesterday. Use only the attached call notes. Keep it under 150 words, sound warm and direct, and end with one question about scheduling. Do not invent a price or promise a completion date. Flag missing information before drafting.

The second prompt works because Claude does not have to guess the goal, facts, tone, length, or safety boundary.

You do not need to write a long prompt every day. Use the comprehensive prompt at the end of this article once to create a small set of reusable prompts for your business.

## Minutes 25–35: write Project instructions

**Project instructions** are the customer-facing way to tell Claude how to work inside a Project. They are plain-English directions that Claude applies to every chat in that Project, so you do not need to repeat the same business context, writing preferences, and approval rules each time.

A practical instruction set might say:

- Write for customers who are knowledgeable about their problem but not our process.
- Use short paragraphs and direct language.
- Use only attached or cited sources for factual claims.
- Separate verified facts from recommendations.
- Never invent prices, availability, testimonials, or results.
- Ask before sending, publishing, purchasing, deleting, or changing external information.
- End public-facing drafts with a human-review checklist.

Project instructions should describe stable rules, not today’s task. Put “always write in our approved tone” in the instructions. Put “draft Tuesday’s customer email” in the chat.

## Minutes 35–45: create something visual with Claude Design

Claude Design is a visual workspace where you describe what you need, review a draft on a canvas, and refine it through conversation or direct editing.

A nontechnical owner might use it to create:

- A one-page service overview
- A workshop flyer
- A five-slide social carousel
- A sales presentation
- A simple webpage concept
- An internal process diagram

Claude Design is currently a beta product on supported paid plans. Anthropic’s [Claude Design overview](https://claude.com/product/design) lists current availability and export destinations.

Give Claude Design approved copy, exact contact details, your logo, colors, fonts, final dimensions, and the action the reader should take. Do not ask it to invent the business information while it designs the business information.

For a complete walkthrough and main design prompt, read [What Is Claude Design?](/blog/posts/how-to-create-share-design-claude-design/).

## Minutes 45–52: understand Skills and connectors

These two features solve different problems.

### A Skill teaches the procedure

A Skill captures how you perform a repeated task: the steps, decision rules, output format, examples, exceptions, and moments when Claude must stop for approval.

Examples include:

- Turn a transcript into your meeting-note format
- Review marketing copy against your brand checklist
- Prepare a weekly report
- Categorize customer feedback

Anthropic describes [Skills](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12512176-what-are-skills) as reusable instructions and resources that Claude loads when relevant. Start with one small process you can test, not a universal Skill that tries to operate the entire company.

### A connector provides access

A connector lets Claude retrieve information or take supported actions in services such as Google Drive, Gmail, or Slack. It does not automatically give Claude a good procedure.

Before connecting anything, check:

- What can it read?
- What can it change?
- Which account permissions will it inherit?
- When will it ask for approval?
- Can you test it read-only first?

Anthropic’s [connector guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11176164-use-connectors-to-extend-claude-s-capabilities) notes that connectors inherit the user’s permissions in the connected service. Access should be narrow, necessary, and understood.

For a deeper comparison, read [Claude Skills Explained](/blog/posts/what-are-claude-skills/).

## Minutes 52–57: know when to use Cowork

Chat helps with a task. Cowork is for delegating a larger outcome that may require several steps, files, tools, or workstreams.

Good Cowork assignments have:

- An exact outcome
- Approved sources
- A defined output and save location
- Actions Claude may and may not take
- A visible definition of done
- A human approval point before consequential changes

Cowork can create plans, coordinate work, use approved files or connectors, and deliver finished documents. Availability and execution modes vary by plan and surface; Anthropic’s [Cowork getting-started guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13345190-get-started-with-claude-cowork) has the current details.

Do not begin by giving Cowork broad access and a vague objective. Start with read-only source material and a reversible output, such as turning approved meeting notes into an action register.

Read [What Are Claude and Cowork?](/blog/posts/what-is-claude-and-cowork/) for a safer first delegation prompt.

## Minutes 57–60: test before you expand

Run one real task and score the finished result:

- Was every factual claim supported?
- How much editing did it require?
- Did it follow the requested format?
- Did it respect the approval boundary?
- Did it save meaningful time after review?
- Would you trust the same process on another low-risk example?

If the task fails, improve the context, sources, or instructions before adding another feature. A connector does not fix a vague process. Cowork does not fix an unclear finish line. A larger prompt does not fix missing facts.

## What should you ignore for now?

You do not need a custom Skill on day one. You do not need to connect every account. You do not need Cowork for a task one chat can finish. You do not need Claude Design for a document that only needs clean text.

Start with this stack:

1. One Project
2. One useful source set
3. One stable instruction set
4. Three reusable prompts
5. One low-risk weekly workflow

Add another feature only when it removes a specific repeated problem.

## Comprehensive prompt: set up Claude for your business

Create a new Claude conversation and paste the prompt below. Claude will interview you, choose one practical first workflow, draft your Project instructions, and build a seven-day test plan. Do not include sensitive business information during the interview.

```text
Act as a practical Claude setup coach for a nontechnical business owner.
Help me create one useful, safe Claude workflow without adding features I
do not need.

INTERVIEW ME FIRST
Ask one focused question at a time. Learn:
- what my business does and who we serve
- the repeated tasks that consume the most time
- which task I want to improve first
- how the task works today
- the source material required
- what a good finished result looks like
- common mistakes and exceptions
- sensitive information involved
- actions that require human approval
- how I will measure whether the workflow saves time or improves quality

Do not ask for passwords, private customer records, regulated data, or
confidential information. Use placeholders when an example would require
sensitive material.

CHOOSE THE SIMPLEST CLAUDE SETUP
After the interview, recommend only what the first workflow needs:
- ordinary Chat for a one-time task
- a Project for reusable background and instructions
- Claude Design for visual output
- a Skill for a stable repeated procedure
- a connector for necessary approved access
- Cowork for a bounded multi-step outcome

Explain why each recommended feature is necessary. Put every unnecessary
feature in a “Not yet” list.

BUILD THE SETUP
Create:
1. A clear name and purpose for the Claude Project, if needed.
2. A checklist of safe, current source material to add.
3. Complete Project instructions covering audience, tone, trusted sources,
   factual accuracy, output preferences, privacy boundaries, and actions
   requiring approval.
4. Three reusable prompts for the first workflow: create, review, and
   revise.
5. One comprehensive workflow prompt that takes the task from source material to
   a finished draft and human-review checklist.
6. A Skill outline only if the process is stable and repeated.
7. A connector permission checklist only if external access is necessary.
8. A Cowork delegation brief only if the work is genuinely multi-step.
9. A Claude Design brief only if the final output needs visual design.

TEST IT
Create a seven-day rollout plan using old, sample, or low-risk work first.
Define:
- the baseline time and quality
- three test cases, including one incomplete or conflicting input
- what Claude may do
- what always requires my approval
- the success metric
- the stop condition

FINISH WITH
1. A one-page setup summary.
2. The exact order in which I should configure and test everything.
3. A daily checklist requiring no more than 10 minutes.
4. A list of assumptions and unanswered questions.
5. A reminder of what I must personally verify before using the output.

Prefer the smallest reliable workflow. Do not recommend AI, automation,
connectors, Skills, Design, or Cowork merely because they are available.
```

The goal of this bootcamp is not to master Claude in an hour. It is to leave with one useful system you understand well enough to supervise.
