Real estate agents do not need twenty AI subscriptions. They need one tool they understand and a few reliable workflows attached to work they already do.

Claude can handle most of the planning, writing, repurposing, and review in one place. Claude Design can create the static visuals. You may still use a video editor to finish recordings and a scheduling tool to publish, but you do not need to move your ideas through five different AI products.

Here are seven practical ways to use Claude in 2026.

## 1. Create one Claude Project for your business

Start by creating a Claude Project for your real estate marketing. Add material Claude can safely reuse:

- A short description of your audience and market
- Three to five examples of writing that sounds like you
- Your preferred tone and phrases you avoid
- Your logo, brand colors, fonts, and approved headshot
- Brokerage advertising requirements
- A blank property fact-sheet template
- A blank market-update source template

Projects keep instructions and supporting material together, so you do not have to explain your voice from scratch in every conversation. Keep confidential client information and licensed data out unless your brokerage has approved the tool and the specific use.

## 2. Draft listing descriptions from verified facts

Give Claude a property fact sheet rather than asking it to invent what makes a home interesting. It can produce several positioning angles, shorten the strongest version to an MLS limit, and compare every claim with the facts you provided.

Claude should never be the source for square footage, renovation dates, school assignments, HOA information, or property features. It is the writer. You remain the verifier and publisher.

For the complete process, see [how to write real estate listing descriptions with Claude](/blog/posts/write-real-estate-listing-descriptions-with-ai/).

## 3. Turn showing notes into personal follow-up

After a showing, add a short note such as:

> The buyers liked the kitchen and backyard, worried about the upstairs carpet, and want disclosures before deciding on a second visit.

Claude can turn that into a concise follow-up email, a CRM note, and a task list. Because it starts with your notes, the draft can reflect the actual conversation instead of sounding like a generic sequence.

Review the message before sending it. Claude should not add promises, deadlines, property claims, or advice you did not provide.

## 4. Create a month of social content

Give Claude one reliable source: an approved market report, a frequently asked client question, an upcoming open house, or verified listing information. It can turn that source into buyer education, seller education, local-market explanations, captions, carousel copy, and short video scripts.

The useful part is not producing thirty random captions. It is building a small content system from information you already trust. The full workflow is in [how to create a month of real estate content with Claude](/blog/posts/month-of-real-estate-social-content-with-ai/).

## 5. Create static graphics with Claude Design

Claude Design can turn a written brief into social graphics, carousels, flyers, and presentations. Add your approved assets, describe the format, and refine the design through conversation or direct canvas editing.

Anthropic describes Claude Design as a beta workspace for creating designs, decks, and prototypes. Its [official Claude Design overview](https://claude.com/product/design) lists the current plans, editing controls, and export options.

You can send work to Canva when you need its templates or exact resizing. Claude’s [Canva connector](https://claude.com/connectors/canva) can also create, resize, brand-check, and export Canva designs from Claude. That makes Canva an optional finishing layer rather than a separate place to restart the project.

## 6. Build market-update videos before recording

Upload an approved market report and ask Claude to identify the few changes a homeowner needs to understand. It can produce a source-based outline, a 60-second script, an email version, and a caption from the same information.

Claude can plan the video and create a title card or supporting graphic in Claude Design. You will still need to record it. A video editor or the social platform’s editor is usually the simplest way to trim the clip, correct captions, and export the finished video.

## 7. Review content before it leaves your desk

Claude can perform a structured second review:

- Compare claims with your source material
- Flag unsupported or ambiguous wording
- Identify absolute claims and predictions
- Find phrases that may need Fair Housing review
- Check the requested length and format
- Produce a final human-review checklist

That review is useful, but it is not approval. The National Association of REALTORS® recommends reviewing AI-generated content for accuracy and reminds agents that they remain responsible for honest communications. Its [AI risk-reduction guidance](https://www.nar.realtor/legal/hot-topics-for-brokers) is a helpful starting point. HUD also prohibits housing advertisements that indicate preferences or limitations based on protected characteristics; review its [Fair Housing rights and obligations](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_rights_and_obligations).

## What still happens outside Claude?

Claude can handle nearly all of the thinking and first-draft production. Three final steps may still happen elsewhere:

1. Record and edit videos.
2. Schedule or publish posts.
3. Complete brokerage, MLS, legal, or compliance approval.

That is a manageable stack: **Claude for the work, Claude Design for static visuals, and your existing publishing tools for the final mile.**

## One prompt to run your real estate marketing workflow

Create a Claude Project, add the safe reference material listed above, then paste this prompt whenever you have new verified notes. Delete any deliverable you do not need.

```text
Act as my real estate marketing assistant. Use only the project material
and verified source notes I provide. Do not use outside facts or fill in
missing property, market, client, or legal details.

ABOUT ME
Market or service area: [AREA]
Audience: [BUYERS, SELLERS, HOMEOWNERS, OR OTHER]
Tone: helpful, direct, personal, and professional
Brokerage requirements: [PASTE OR REFER TO PROJECT FILE]

VERIFIED SOURCE NOTES
[PASTE PROPERTY FACTS, MARKET REPORT NOTES, SHOWING NOTES, OPEN-HOUSE
DETAILS, OR CLIENT FAQ]

FIRST:
1. Identify contradictions, missing information, and anything that needs
   human verification.
2. Ask me no more than five necessary questions before drafting. If the
   material is sufficient, continue without questions.

THEN CREATE ONLY THE DELIVERABLES SUPPORTED BY MY NOTES:
1. Three listing-description angles, each within [CHARACTER LIMIT].
2. A personal follow-up email under 150 words with one clear next step.
3. A CRM note and task list based on the same conversation.
4. A 12-post monthly content plan: three buyer posts, three seller posts,
   three local-market posts, and three behind-the-scenes posts.
5. For the four strongest posts, write the finished caption, a five-slide
   carousel, and a 60-second video script.
6. A concise seller or market-update email when the notes support one.
7. A visual brief for each static asset. If this is running in Claude
   Design, create the graphics using my approved brand assets.

RULES:
- Never invent a fact, statistic, feature, date, quote, or source.
- Describe properties and services, not preferred types of people.
- Avoid predictions, pressure, hype, and generic phrases such as
  “dream home,” “perfect for,” and “won't last.”
- Separate verified facts from my opinions.
- Flag wording that needs Fair Housing, licensing, MLS, brokerage, or
  legal review; do not claim that content is compliant.
- Keep my voice natural. Do not use excessive emojis or exclamation marks.

FINISH WITH:
1. A table matching every factual public claim to its source note.
2. A short checklist of what I must verify before publishing.
3. A recommended order for completing and publishing the deliverables.
```

Claude will do most of the production, but the safest instruction in the prompt is also the simplest: **do not invent missing information.**
