# Phase 2: Event Design

Event: **AI for Business Raleigh**
Host: Danny Sullivan (dannysullivan.ai)
Audience: non-technical Raleigh small business owners and entrepreneurs. No code on any screen, ever.

## The format (locked)

90 minutes, monthly, same venue, same slot. Modeled on the proven small-market monthly meetup playbook: consistent cadence, one live demo, one guest, structured networking, one clear CTA.

### Recurring slot (one recommendation)

**Fourth Wednesday of the month, 5:30 to 7:00 PM.**

Why: small business owners can leave the shop or office by 5, Wednesday avoids Monday chaos and Thursday/Friday social calendars, and 7:00 PM hard stop respects family time. Evening beats lunch because owners who work a counter cannot leave midday.

### Run of show (90 minutes)

| Time | Segment | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 5:30 - 5:55 | Doors, check-in, name tags, food, open networking | Danny greets at the door, works the room |
| 5:55 - 6:00 | Welcome: what this event is, who is in the room, house rules (no pitching, no jargon) | Danny |
| 6:00 - 6:20 | **Live demo**: Danny screen-shares a real business workflow and fixes it with AI in front of everyone | Danny |
| 6:20 - 6:40 | Guest speaker or mini-workshop (non-technical, 15 min + 5 Q&A) | Guest |
| 6:40 - 6:55 | Structured networking: "turn to the person next to you, share the one task you wish AI could take off your plate this week" then 2 or 3 people share out loud | Danny facilitates |
| 6:55 - 7:00 | CTA close (script below) and next-month date announcement | Danny |

The structured networking prompt doubles as lead intelligence: the share-outs tell you exactly who has a pain worth a follow-up.

## The 60-second CTA close (script, verbatim)

> "Before we wrap, here is how I can help beyond this room, and then I will get out of your way.
>
> If tonight sparked something, the easiest next step is free. I do a 30-minute AI workflow review. You bring me one process you wish would disappear, lead follow-up, email, scheduling, whatever eats your week. You leave with one practical recommendation and a first step, even if the answer is not AI. No pitch, no obligation. The link is on the slide and in the follow-up email.
>
> If you already know you want a full picture, I do a paid AI Assessment for 999 dollars. I go deep on your business, map where AI actually saves you time or makes you money, and hand you a prioritized plan. It is tailored to whatever you care about most.
>
> And if you want your whole team on the same page, I run private AI workshops for teams, starting at 750.
>
> That is it. Same room, fourth Wednesday next month. Bring another business owner who complains about their inbox. Good night."

Rules for the close: never more than 60 seconds, never before the value is delivered, always ends on the next event date so the last word is community, not commerce.

## Event #1 topic (one recommendation)

**Title: "Stop Losing Customers You Never Knew Called: Watch AI Catch Missed Leads, Live"**

Format: pure live screen-share, zero slides after the title card. Danny plays a Raleigh business owner for 20 minutes:

1. A customer calls, nobody answers, the call goes to an AI receptionist that captures what they need and texts the owner a summary (Danny has built exactly this at Raleigh Lead Recovery, so the demo is real and credible, not a canned vendor pitch).
2. An email inquiry sits in an inbox; AI drafts the reply and a follow-up sequence in the owner's voice.
3. The kicker: AI tallies what those recovered leads are worth per month in dollars.

Why this topic: it is about money, not technology. Every attendee has missed a call. It naturally demonstrates what a free workflow review, a $999 assessment, or a build engagement would uncover in their own business, without saying so. It also plants Danny's flag: the person in Raleigh who makes AI catch revenue you were already losing.

Backup topic (only if the venue's wifi cannot support live calls): "One Owner's Week, Automated: Watch Me Take 5 Hours of Admin Off a Real Business in 20 Minutes."

## 6-month speaker pipeline

Rule zero, the non-technical test: **if a slide has code on it, they do not speak.** Every speaker demos or teaches something an owner can use Monday morning.

Source speakers from complementary, non-competing services. They get a room full of their exact prospects; you get free content and their audience promotes the event. Never book a competing AI consultant.

| Month | Speaker profile | Why complementary |
|---|---|---|
| Sep (event #2) | Local bookkeeper or fractional CFO: "AI in your books: what to automate, what to never automate" | Finance pros reach owners; zero overlap with Danny's services |
| Oct (event #3) | Marketing agency owner: "AI content that does not sound like AI" | They want owner clients; validates AI-for-marketing demand |
| Nov | Employment attorney or HR consultant: "AI and your employees: policy in plain English" | Compliance angle owners worry about |
| Dec | Panel: 3 attendees who implemented something from a previous event | Social proof flywheel; costs nothing |
| Jan | Commercial insurance or IT services owner: "What AI means for your risk" | Trust-based referral professions |
| Feb | Local operator case study: a restaurant, trades, or retail owner using AI daily | Peer proof beats expert proof |

Sourcing method, in order of yield: (1) attendees who impress during share-outs, (2) the venue's own member base, (3) Raleigh Chamber and Shop Local Raleigh member directories, (4) LinkedIn search "Raleigh" + profession, DM using the personal-invite template with a speaker twist. Book speakers 2 events out, always have one backup confirmed. Speakers get: 20 minutes, their logo on the recap email, a tagged LinkedIn post. Speakers never get: the attendee list.
