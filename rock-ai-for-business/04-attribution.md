# Phase 4: Attribution and Lead Capture

Attribution starts at event #1. Every attendee gets a source tag and an intent tag. No exceptions, or the "1 closed client by Oct 31" measurable cannot be proven.

## Luma registration form (under 60 seconds)

Fields, in order:

1. Name (required, built-in)
2. Email (required, built-in)
3. Company or business name (required, short text)
4. What do you do? (required, one line: "Tell us in one sentence, e.g. 'I own a 6-person landscaping company'")
5. What is the #1 thing you want AI to fix in your business? (required, one line. This is the money question; it pre-scores every lead before they walk in.)
6. How did you hear about this event? (required, dropdown: LinkedIn / Facebook group (which one?) / Meetup.com / A friend or colleague / Danny invited me personally / Chamber or business org / Other)
7. Role (optional dropdown: Owner or founder / Leadership / Employee / Just curious)

Six required fields, all one-liners. Tested completion time is 45 to 60 seconds. Do not add more; every extra field costs registrations.

## Day-of check-in process (solo-friendly)

1. Luma check-in QR on a stand at the door; Danny greets and taps people in on the Luma host app while saying hello. Checking in personally is the point: the 10 seconds of "so what do you do?" at the door is your highest-value data collection.
2. Name tags: first name + business name, hand-written on arrival (pre-print for pre-registrations if time allows).
3. Walk-ins: register on the spot via the QR (they hit the same form, so attribution holds).
4. During share-outs (6:40 segment), keep the Luma attendee list open and jot a 3-word pain note next to anyone who speaks.

## Lead-scoring rubric

Score every attendee within 24 hours. Three buckets:

**HOT** (services lead): fits the services ICP (owner or decision-maker of a real business), AND named a specific pain that maps to a paid engagement, OR asked about working together. Map each HOT lead to the service it points at:
- **Assessment ($999)**: broad pain ("I know we are wasting time everywhere"), wants a plan
- **Free review → assessment**: single concrete workflow pain (missed calls, inbox, scheduling, follow-up)
- **Team workshop ($750+)**: pain is "my team does not get it" or "we need everyone trained"
- **Lead recovery / build work**: pain is missed calls or lost leads specifically (Raleigh Lead Recovery adjacent)

**WARM** (learner): curious, wants to do it themselves, no near-term budget or authority signal. These feed the newsletter and future events, and some ripen into HOT.

**NURTURE**: everyone else, including students, job seekers, vendors, and the merely curious.

## What gets logged in Notion within 24 hours of each event

One database, one row per attendee, logged by Danny (solo op) the morning after:

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| Name / Email / Company | from Luma |
| Event | #1 Aug 26 / #2 Sep 23 / #3 Oct 28 |
| Source | the "how did you hear" answer, verbatim |
| Stated pain | their #1-thing-to-fix answer plus anything said in the room |
| Score | HOT / WARM / NURTURE |
| Service map (HOT only) | Assessment / Free review / Workshop / Build |
| Follow-up sent | date each track email went out |
| Outcome | Review booked / Assessment sold / Workshop booked / Client closed / None yet |

Rule: HOT leads get their personal email the same evening, before scoring the rest. The rubric exists so the Rock's lagging metrics (HOT leads by service type, discovery calls, assessments sold, client closed) fall straight out of this table with zero extra work.
